# Concept: Category (Category Theory)
***
## Overview / Definition
%% 1-2 sentence simple definition of the concept. %%
A **category** is a collection of "objects" which are linked by "arrows" (morphisms), such that morphisms can be composed associatively, and there is an arrow from an object to itself called the "identity morphism". 

A simple example is the category $\mathbf{Sets}$ whose objects are [[../Inbox/Set|sets]], and morphisms are [[Functions|functions]].

## Core principles / Key Characteristics
%% What is important to remember? %%

### Describing a category
A category $\mathcal{C}$ consists of:
- A class (or set) $\operatorname{Ob}(\mathcal{C})$ of **objects**,
- A class (or set) $\operatorname{Mor}$




- A category should technically be specified by its objects, morphisms, composition law and identity morphism. But often it's sufficient to just describe the objects (and maybe morphisms), with the rest coming from context.
- Note that the "identity" of the category refers to the identity morphism, not an object.

## Examples
- $\mathbf{Sets}$, whose objects are sets and morphisms are functions.
- $\mathbf{Groups}$, whose objects are groups and morphisms are group homomorphisms.
- $\mathbf{Rings}$, whose objects are rings and morphisms are ring homomorphisms.
	- $\mathbf{CRings}$, commutative unital rings with morphisms being unital ring homomorphisms.
	- $\mathbf{Alg}_R$, algebras over a ring commutative ring $R$ with morphisms being $R$-linear ring homomorphisms.
- $\mathbf{Top}$, whose objects are topological spaces and morphisms are continuous maps.
